# Your mission 💚

---

Build a relevant retail or consumer product in one day. Pick a problem you care about, choose a track and show what your idea can do.

A strong project connects a clear problem, a thoughtful approach, a working product and evidence that it works.

## ‣ Track 1: Human Truth

### Why do people choose what they choose?

Almost no one buys a product for one simple reason. Price and features matter, but so do habit, trust, taste, identity, convenience and what someone is trying to achieve in that moment.

Build something that **uses AI to understand or predict how people choose, trust or reject products, and turns that understanding into something useful someone can act on**.

Behavioural science is a powerful lens here: habit, trust, loss aversion, social proof and what people are trying to achieve.

You could explore:

- Why people say they value one thing but buy another.
- What someone nearly chose or rejected, and what that gap reveals about the choice they finally made.
- What makes someone trust a brand they’ve never heard of.
- The shopping mission hiding behind a basket.
- Why the same person behaves differently online, in-store or in a different retail environment.
- How AI shopping agents choose differently from humans, and what that means for brands.

**A good fit if:** your product uses human behaviour to explain or predict a choice and enables a useful action, experience or decision.

## ‣ Track 2: Retail Futures

### What should retail do next?

Retail is a chain of bets: what to launch, stock, price, promote or remove, and which brands deserve attention.

Build something that helps a retailer or brand **make a better decision, or spots a decision they should be making but aren't**.

A retail environment is not just a store. It is the people, location, channel, range, price point, mission and context in which a choice happens.

You could explore:

- Finding the next breakout challenger brand before buyers do.
- Understanding why the same product, range or strategy might work in one retail environment but fail in another.
- How store format, location, channel and the people who shop there should change what gets stocked or promoted.
- Finding products people want that nobody is making yet.
- Using trends, behaviour and signals from the wider world to **generate, test or validate the next product worth making**.
- Testing what happens when a product is launched, removed or moved into a different retail environment.

**A good fit if:** your project tells a buyer, brand or retailer what to do next, and why.

These are starting points. You are welcome to explore ideas we have not listed.

**Go beyond the obvious data.**
We’re especially interested in projects that combine and synthesise signals in thoughtful ways, discover useful datasets or sources we haven’t considered, and can clearly explain the **logic, assumptions or maths** behind why the evidence leads to the conclusion.

## What we are not looking for

Across both tracks, a project should do more than summarise information or wrap a single prompt.

<aside>
⚠️

- Examples that are not enough on their own
    - Generic chatbots, AI personal shoppers, retail copilots or “chat with this report” interfaces.
    - Basic sentiment analysis, review summaries or transcript-to-persona generators.
    - Generic recommenders, static dashboards, trend reports or market summaries.
    - Basic web-search agents or a single LLM call wrapped in a UI.
    
    These can all be parts of a stronger solution. Show the insight, workflow or decision your product enables, and why it matters.
    
</aside>

<aside>
💡

**Think beyond the demo.**
You only have one day, so we don’t expect production-ready infrastructure. But strong projects should show an understanding of what it would take to make the idea real beyond the hack: scale, privacy, security, cost, data ownership, limitations and other real-world constraints.

</aside>

## Judging criteria

All projects use the same criteria and weights:

| **Criterion** | **Weight** | **What we look for** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Originality & Thinking | 30% | An interesting, non-obvious idea or approach that brings a fresh perspective. |
| Build & Execution | 30% | A working product with meaningful technical or product depth. Show that the core functionality works and that you’ve considered how the approach could become robust, scalable and secure beyond the demo. |
| Value & Relevance | 25% | A useful solution to a relevant problem for people, brands or retail, with evidence to support its claims. |
| Demo & Communication | 15% | A clear demonstration of what you built, why it matters and what works today. |

### How judging works

**Stage 1: Finalist selection**

After the 17:30 submission deadline, judges will assess submissions using the published judging criteria. The **top three projects from Human Truth** and the **top three projects from Retail Futures** will advance to the live final.

**Stage 2: Live final**

The six finalist teams will each have maximum 3 minutes to present their project live, with up to 2 minutes of Q&A.

Finalists will then be assessed again using the **same judging criteria**. **Stage 1 scores do not carry over to the final.** Final-round scores and judge deliberation will determine the Overall Winner and both Track Winners.

If scores are tied or judges require further discussion, the judging panel will make the final decision.
